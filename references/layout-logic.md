# Physical and logical layout rules

The owner reads slides as physical scenes: an arrow, a badge or a light must behave as it would in the real world and must mean something. Every decorative element answers "what does this say, and does its position say it?".

## Direction and order
- RTL decks read right to left: sequences, timelines, chevrons and "next" arrows run from right to left; step 01 is at the right.
- Growth rises toward the reading end: a staircase of levels or stages climbs from the smallest at the start edge (right in RTL) to the biggest at the other end. Keep every chart, bar and staircase in the deck rising the same way.
- Slide order matches the contents slide: a section listed second comes second.
- A legend or explanation appears before the first thing that uses it (explain the "+" tags at the start of the section, not on the slide after).

## Arrows, badges, connectors
- A "next level" or "recommended" arrow travels through empty space above the cards and lands on the top of the target card. Never loop an arrow under or behind cards.
- A badge such as "Recommended" sits on the top edge of its card like a crown, half outside, not inside the text flow.
- Connectors start and end on the thing they connect: payment pins sit on a line under the timeline exactly at the phase boundary they belong to, with a dashed vertical link through the gap between task columns.

## Shapes and depth
- In a staircase, give the cards equal heights so both tops and bottoms step evenly; uneven heights break the stair.
- Front objects are larger and brighter; objects behind are smaller and dimmer. Nothing important hides behind another object.
- One light direction per slide: cards lit from the bottom, glows from the edges, metal surfaces (silver, gold, platinum cards) with highlights from the same side.
- A hero object may overlap the hero word only while every letter stays readable.

## Collisions and fit
- No text over text, no text under a phone, no label running into a neighbour (check centred labels near the slide edges; align the first one right and the last one left).
- Nothing overflows its card or the slide; long lines wrap inside their column, and a full column is shortened, not shrunk below 20 px.
- A slide that is half empty under its content is resized to fill the space or the content is centred; one dominant element per slide.
