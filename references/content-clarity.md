# Text any reader can follow

Applies when Claude writes or edits the deck's text. Picture the least prepared reader of this audience (for a business deck: a 55-year-old who is not technical and reads slowly) and the most demanding one (a young expert). Both must get the point of every slide without the presenter.

## Words
- Match the language and register the user asked for (Arabic decks often in simple formal Arabic, فصحى بسيطة). Messages to the user stay in his own dialect.
- Replace jargon with everyday words. A term the audience must learn is explained once, at first use. Arabic replacements proven in review:

| Avoid | Write |
|---|---|
| بروفايل | الملف الشخصي |
| الشات | المحادثة |
| السيرفر (for non-technical readers) | الخادم، أو اشرحه مرة |
| الكود المصدري | ملفات البرمجة الأصلية |
| شغّالة / افتراضيًا | تعمل / تلقائيًا |
| الترويج / التراجع (app features) | إظهار الملف لعدد أكبر / الرجوع لملف تم تخطيه |

- One name per thing across the whole deck. A tag, label or legend uses exactly the name of what it points to.
- Check the spelling of names and add diacritics where a word has two readings (قَسَم versus قسم).
- Latin product names inside Arabic stay on one line (non-breaking spaces) and are kept to what the reader needs.
- No em dash or en dash; use a comma, a colon or parentheses.

## Structure
- A contents slide that matches the real order. Each section opens where the contents says it does.
- Each slide: a title that says the point, one short lede sentence, then the evidence (image, list, numbers). Lists of about 4 to 6 items; longer lists are split or moved.
- Explain any symbol, colour code or tag before or at its first use, once.
- Exhaustive material (full lists, technical stacks, raw data) goes to an appendix after the closing slide.
- Technology or vendor names are presented as suggestions unless the user has decided them.

## Numbers and claims
- Every number has its unit and, if it is an estimate, says so.
- The same number reads the same on every slide; cross-check totals, sums, durations and dates between slides before delivery.
- Make comparisons explicit (name a discount or a difference instead of leaving the reader to subtract).
- Say plainly what the reader gets, what it costs or demands of him, and what happens next, when the deck asks him to decide something.

## Borrowed material and trust
- Images from other people's products are labelled as illustration; never let them pass as the author's work.
- A competitor's or reference's name belongs to them; if the author's own item has a similar name, rename the author's item.
- Do not state "we do not copy" next to copied content.
- Blur names, cities, descriptions, phone numbers and real photos of private people.
